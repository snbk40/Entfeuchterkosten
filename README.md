# Entfeuchterkosten

Entfeuchterkosten is a custom Home Assistant integration that calculates the
electricity costs of a dehumidifier ("Entfeuchter") from its power sensor,
for example the one of a smart plug, and a fixed electricity price.

If you have solar panels, for example a balcony solar plant, their power is offset against the dehumidifier's in real
time: solar power is free, so only the part of the dehumidifier's power above
it costs money.

Integration domain: `entfeuchterkosten`

## What you get

For each dehumidifier you set up:

| Sensor | Unit | Starts again at zero |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost today | your currency | every midnight |
| Cost this month | your currency | on the first of every month |
| Cost this year | your currency | on 1 January |
| Total cost | your currency | never |
| Savings today | your currency | every midnight |
| Savings this month | your currency | on the first of every month |
| Savings this year | your currency | on 1 January |
| Total savings | your currency | never |
| Total energy | kWh | never |
| Chargeable power | W | – (current value) |
| Solar savings | W | – (current value) |

The currency is the one set under **Settings > System > General**.

## Requirements

- Home Assistant 2026.9 or newer
- A sensor that measures the power of the dehumidifier in W or kW, for example
  from a smart plug with power measurement
- Optionally, a sensor that measures the power generated by your solar panels,
  for example from the inverter of a balcony solar plant

Entfeuchterkosten needs no extra Python packages.

## Install with HACS

1. In HACS, open the menu at the top right and choose **Custom repositories**.
2. Add `https://github.com/snbk40/Entfeuchterkosten` with the type
   **Integration**.
3. Search for **Entfeuchterkosten** in HACS and download it.
4. Restart Home Assistant, then continue with step 3 of the manual install.

## Install manually

1. Copy the folder `custom_components/entfeuchterkosten` into the
   `custom_components` folder of your Home Assistant configuration.
2. Restart Home Assistant.
3. Go to **Settings > Devices & services > Helpers > Create helper** and choose
   **Entfeuchterkosten**.

## Settings

| Setting | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Name | The name of the device that holds the sensors, for example "Keller-Entfeuchter" |
| Power sensor | The sensor that measures the power of the dehumidifier |
| Solar power sensor | Optional: the sensor that measures the power generated by your solar panels |
| Electricity price | The price of one kWh, including all taxes and fees |

The power sensors and the price can be changed later with **Configure**. A new
price applies from then on; the costs already counted stay as they are. Each
power sensor can be used by only one Entfeuchterkosten entry; set up one entry
per dehumidifier.

## How the costs are counted

- The power is taken to stay at its last value until the power sensor reports
  a new one. That matches a smart plug, whose reading jumps when the compressor
  switches on or off.
- The costs are brought up to date every minute and at every change of the
  power sensor, so a constant power shows up too.
- Energy used across midnight is split: the part before midnight counts for
  the old day, the part after it for the new one. The same goes for months and
  years.
- While the power sensor is *unavailable* or *unknown*, nothing is counted.
  The time while Home Assistant is not running is not counted either.
- Negative power counts as zero.

## Solar power

With a solar power sensor set up, the solar power at each moment is taken off
the dehumidifier's power:

- **Chargeable power** = power of the dehumidifier − solar power, but never
  below zero. It updates with every change of either sensor.
- **Solar savings** = the part of the dehumidifier's power that solar power
  covers: the smaller of the two. Chargeable power and solar savings always add
  up to the dehumidifier's power.
- The costs count only the chargeable power. While the solar panels generate
  more than the dehumidifier uses, it costs nothing.
- The **savings** count the solar savings at the same price: what the energy
  the solar panels covered would have cost from the grid. Costs and savings
  together are what the dehumidifier would cost without solar panels. Like
  the costs, the daily, monthly and yearly savings start again at zero.
- **Total energy** still counts everything the dehumidifier uses, solar or not.
- While the solar power sensor is *unavailable* or *unknown*, as many
  inverters are at night, the solar power counts as zero: the dehumidifier's
  whole power is chargeable.
- All the solar power is assumed to be available to the dehumidifier. If other
  devices in the house use it at the same time, the real costs are higher.
- Without a solar power sensor, the chargeable power is the dehumidifier's
  power and the solar savings are 0 W.
- All values are kept when Home Assistant restarts or the integration is
  reloaded. A daily, monthly or yearly cost whose period ended while Home
  Assistant was not running starts again at zero.

To show the costs in the Energy dashboard, add the dehumidifier under
**Settings > Dashboards > Energy > Individual devices** with **Total energy**.

## Dashboard

[`dashboards/entfeuchterkosten.yaml`](dashboards/entfeuchterkosten.yaml) is a
ready-made dashboard with everything Entfeuchterkosten shows:

- **Now:** a gauge of the chargeable power, the solar savings, the power of
  the dehumidifier and of the solar panels, and all four over the last 24
  hours
- **Costs:** the costs of today, this month, this year and in total, the
  total energy, and today's costs over the day
- **Savings:** the savings of today, this month, this year and in total,
  and a bar chart of the savings per day
- **Statistics:** bar charts of the costs per day and per month, and of the
  energy per day

It uses only cards built into Home Assistant. Its entity IDs are the ones the sensors get when the helper is named
"Entfeuchter", for example `sensor.entfeuchter_cost_today`.

1. The file uses `sensor.entfeuchter_plug_power` as the power sensor of the
   dehumidifier and `sensor.inverter_plug_power` as the solar power sensor. If
   yours are named differently, replace them. Without a solar power sensor, delete the cards that use
   it.
2. If you did not name the helper "Entfeuchter", replace the prefix
   `sensor.entfeuchter_` of the other sensors with the name you chose, in
   lower case. The exact entity IDs are listed on the helper's device page.
3. Go to **Settings > Dashboards > Add dashboard** and choose
   **New dashboard from scratch**. Give it a name, for example
   "Entfeuchterkosten".
4. Open the new dashboard, click the pencil at the top right, then
   **⋮ > Raw configuration editor**.
5. Replace everything in the editor with the contents of the file and click
   **Save**.

The bar charts fill up over time: Home Assistant keeps the hourly statistics
they are drawn from only from the moment the sensors exist.

## How it is built

| File | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| `custom_components/entfeuchterkosten/manifest.json` | Name, domain, version and metadata of the integration |
| `custom_components/entfeuchterkosten/const.py` | Constants shared by all files |
| `custom_components/entfeuchterkosten/cost.py` | Turns power over time into energy and costs. No Home Assistant code |
| `custom_components/entfeuchterkosten/tracker.py` | Follows the power sensors and feeds the cost meter |
| `custom_components/entfeuchterkosten/__init__.py` | Sets the integration up and tears it down |
| `custom_components/entfeuchterkosten/config_flow.py` | The setup dialog and the options dialog |
| `custom_components/entfeuchterkosten/sensor.py` | The cost, energy, chargeable power and solar savings sensors |
| `custom_components/entfeuchterkosten/translations/` | UI texts (English) |
| `dashboards/entfeuchterkosten.yaml` | A ready-made dashboard |
| `hacs.json` | Name and minimum Home Assistant version for HACS |
| `tests/test_cost.py` | Tests of the cost meter |
| `pyproject.toml` | Settings for the code checkers ruff and mypy |

## Tests

`tests/test_cost.py` checks the counting, including the solar offset, midnight, the turn of the
year and the change to winter time. It needs no Home Assistant. On Windows,
install `tzdata` first (`pip install tzdata`).

```
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v
```

The code is checked with [ruff](https://docs.astral.sh/ruff/) and
[mypy](https://mypy-lang.org/), configured in `pyproject.toml`:

```
ruff check .
ruff format --check .
mypy custom_components/entfeuchterkosten tests
```
